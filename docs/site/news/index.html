News

What has happened in this field, and what it means for someone with an Android phone.

  1. October 12, 2026

    Norway's record fine hits Microsoft's Xandr over Grindr users' advertising data

    Norway's regulator fined ad firm Xandr, now Microsoft's, NOK 92 million for relaying Grindr users' data, which can reveal sexual orientation, through ad auctions without valid consent. Grindr's own NOK 65 million fine from 2021 was upheld on appeal in 2025.

    Opening an app can send your data to many ad companies at once, and the fact that you use an app can itself be sensitive.

    Disputed: Microsoft said the investigation concerned conduct before it bought Xandr and that it was reviewing the decision. The regulator found Xandr assumed Grindr had valid consent without checking it, and says Xandr did not appeal, so the decision is final.

    Source: Datatilsynet (Norwegian Data Protection Authority) · BleepingComputer

  2. April 9, 2026

    Citizen Lab details Webloc, a tool that tracks phones through app and ad data

    Citizen Lab reported that Webloc draws on data from consumer apps and digital advertising. Sales and contract documents claim up to 500 million devices and three years of history, which it could not check. Buyers it names include Hungarian intelligence and US agencies such as ICE.

    Location data that apps pass into advertising can end up in tools used by police and intelligence agencies.

    Disputed: Penlink, which sells Webloc, says the findings rest on inaccurate information or a misunderstanding, that its providers obtain consent, and that some products described no longer exist.

    Source: Citizen Lab · The Hacker News

  3. February 17, 2026

    Keenadu backdoor found in validly signed Android tablet firmware

    Kaspersky found a backdoor built into firmware of Alldocube and other, unnamed tablets, in images with valid signatures and some over-the-air updates. It gives remote control and is mainly used for ad fraud. Kaspersky counted 13,715 users who met it or its modules.

    A valid signature did not mean the firmware was clean: the researchers think the code was added while it was being built.

    Disputed: Alldocube earlier acknowledged malware in one model and later issued firmware Kaspersky found clean. Google says the affected Play apps were removed and Play Protect blocks known versions.

    Source: Kaspersky Securelist · BleepingComputer

  4. January 29, 2026

    Google disrupts IPIDEA, a proxy network built on ordinary apps

    Google took legal action against IPIDEA and identified over 600 Android apps whose code connected to it, often through SDKs that paid developers per download. Their users' phones could carry strangers' traffic. Google says Play Protect on certified devices now warns about and removes apps known to use them.

    An app you chose for something else can lend your connection to others, and that traffic is tied to your address.

    Disputed: IPIDEA denies ever running or controlling a botnet and says Google did not contact it first. It accepts that open networks can be abused and that some resellers do not fully apply its customer checks.

    Source: Google Threat Intelligence Group · iTnews

  5. November 7, 2025

    LANDFALL spyware targeted Samsung Galaxy phones through a flaw in image handling

    Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 found Android spyware sent in malformed image files that used a Samsung flaw, patched in April 2025. Samples date from July 2024. Its code points to recording, location, photo and message theft. Delivery may have needed no tap; this is unconfirmed.

    The flaw was in Samsung's own image library, and the fix reached phones only through a security update.

    Disputed: Unit 42 has not attributed it to any vendor or government. The developer and the number of victims are unknown.

    Source: Unit 42, Palo Alto Networks · TechCrunch

  6. October 13, 2025

    Pixnapping: an Android app with no permissions could read other apps' screens

    University researchers showed that a malicious app with no permissions can rebuild what other apps display, pixel by pixel, including two-factor codes, on Pixel 6 to 9 and Galaxy S25 phones. Google's September 2025 patch was bypassed, and a further fix was planned.

    It needs no permission, so a permission list would not reveal it. The researchers say they do not know if real attacks use it.

    Disputed: Google says the technique needs specific data about the target device, which lowers its success rate. No malicious apps using it were found on Google Play.

    Source: Pixnapping researchers · BleepingComputer

  7. August 14, 2025

    Study links VPN apps with over 700 million downloads into three families

    Researchers tied Android VPN apps with over 700 million combined Google Play downloads into three families that look like separate companies. Some share hard-coded passwords that the paper says could let an attacker decrypt traffic.

    A VPN app sees all your traffic, and a store listing may not show who runs it or how well it protects you.

    Disputed: Help Net Security later clarified that not all studied providers deliberately hide ownership. Separately, the developer of Snap VPN told the Tech Transparency Project in 2025 that it operates independently and has no affiliation with Qihoo 360.

    Source: Mixon-Baca, Knockel and Crandall (FOCI 2025) · Help Net Security

  8. July 2, 2025

    Stalkerware app Catwatchful exposes data from 26,000 victims' phones

    A researcher found a flaw exposing about 62,000 customer logins of this hidden Android app, which takes photos, messages, location and live audio. TechCrunch reported phone data from about 26,000 victims and called it at least the fifth spyware operation with a data spill that year.

    These apps hide on the phone, and the private data they take can then leak from the seller's own servers.

    Disputed: The seller markets it as child monitoring software. The operator did not reply to TechCrunch.

    Source: TechCrunch · Eric Daigle (the researcher)

  9. July 1, 2025

    Jury orders Google to pay $314.6 million over data Android phones sent while idle

    A California jury found Google liable for Android phones exchanging data with it over users' cellular plans while idle, without permission, and awarded $314.6 million. A similar nationwide case, Taylor v. Google, reached a $135 million settlement in 2026.

    The case concerns data sent in the background on a plan the user pays for, not data the user chose to send.

    Disputed: Google said it would appeal, and that the transfers serve security and performance, use little data and have user consent. We could not confirm what has happened to the verdict since.

    Source: Reuters, via The Star · Bloomberg Law

  10. June 5, 2025

    FBI warns of BADBOX 2.0: millions of home devices infected, some before purchase

    The FBI said millions of devices such as TV boxes and projectors joined the BADBOX 2.0 botnet through software set up before sale or apps required at setup. Google, which says the botnet infected over 10 million uncertified Android devices, sued the operators.

    Such software can be there before you unbox a device, and infected devices end up in proxy services that criminals use.

    Disputed: Google's claims about the operators are allegations in its lawsuit.

    Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center · Google

  11. June 3, 2025

    Facebook, Instagram and Yandex apps tied web browsing to users via a local channel

    Researchers found Meta and Yandex scripts on websites passing identifiers to the companies' Android apps through the phone's own local ports, tying web visits to the user, even in Incognito mode. Both stopped after the disclosure in June 2025, the researchers say.

    No Android permission was needed, so nothing on the phone's permission screens showed this link.

    Disputed: Meta said it was talking with Google about a possible miscommunication over its policies and paused the feature. Yandex told Sky News it does not track Android users' web browsing and complies with data protection standards.

    Source: Local Mess researchers (IMDEA Networks, KU Leuven, Radboud University) · The Register

  12. January 31, 2025

    WhatsApp warns about 90 people of Paragon spyware; traces found on Android phones

    WhatsApp notified about 90 accounts it believed were targeted with Paragon's Graphite, including journalists and civil society members, after stopping an attack that needed no tap. Citizen Lab later reported signs of infection on Android phones in Italy.

    Citizen Lab notes Android's logs are sporadic, so finding no trace does not prove a phone was never infected.

    Disputed: Paragon's chairman said Citizen Lab's summary had several inaccuracies, without naming them. Italy's government first denied knowledge, then confirmed it was a Paragon customer and that its intelligence service had used Graphite, and denied targeting journalists.

    Source: Citizen Lab · The Hacker News

  13. January 7, 2025

    Hack of Gravy Analytics shows how location data from apps reaches brokers

    Hackers claimed to have stolen customer lists and smartphone location data from the broker Gravy Analytics, which later confirmed a breach. A lawsuit says the coordinates came through apps including Tinder, Grindr and Candy Crush.

    Location data from everyday apps can end up with companies the user has never heard of, and then with hackers.

    Disputed: Gravy says it does not track users itself and licenses the data from brokers and aggregators. The 17 TB size is the hacker's claim, and the app list comes from a lawsuit.

    Source: 404 Media · The Register

  14. July 16, 2024

    A data broker's free sample held 3.6 billion location points from phones in Germany

    Journalists at netzpolitik.org and Bayerischer Rundfunk got a free sample from broker Datastream with about 11 million device IDs over two months. They could trace individuals, including people who apparently work for ministries, the military and intelligence services.

    Location data from apps such as weather, navigation or dating apps can be bought, and movements can reveal who someone is.

    Disputed: Datastream and Datarade did not respond. The journalists say real device numbers are probably under 11 million and that the apps behind this data are unknown.

    Source: netzpolitik.org · The Record

  15. March 26, 2024

    Free VPN apps on Google Play secretly turned phones into proxies

    HUMAN's researchers listed 28 Google Play apps, 17 of them free VPNs, that turned Android devices into proxy nodes through a monetisation SDK from LumiApps. Google removed all 28 and said Play Protect disables them.

    A free VPN can use your connection for other people's traffic, and the signs may be only slow internet or blocked sites.

    Disputed: The link to proxy seller Asocks is HUMAN's own assessment, and it is unclear how much the apps' developers knew.

    Source: BleepingComputer (on HUMAN's research) · Malwarebytes

  16. January 9, 2024

    FTC bars X-Mode from selling sensitive location data gathered through apps

    An order proposed in January 2024 and finalised that April bars X-Mode (now Outlogic) from selling sensitive location data gathered through its SDK in other apps, its own apps and other brokers. The FTC reached similar orders with Gravy Analytics and, in a 2026 proposed settlement, Kochava.

    The FTC said an SDK inside ordinary apps fed this data, and that X-Mode did not reliably honour some Android users' opt-outs.

    Disputed: Outlogic said it disagreed with the implications of the FTC's release and that the FTC found no misuse of data.

    Source: US Federal Trade Commission · BleepingComputer

  17. December 6, 2023

    US senator says governments sought phone push-notification records

    Senator Ron Wyden said his office was tipped off in 2022 that foreign government agencies were demanding push notification records from Apple and Google. The records show which app got a notification, when, and for which phone and account. He named no governments.

    Notifications pass through the phone system's provider, so records of which apps you use can exist outside your phone.

    Disputed: Apple and Google told Wyden's office they were barred from publicly releasing push notification records in the United States. The letter names no foreign governments.

    Source: Benton Institute (on Senator Wyden's letter) · UPI

  18. May 29, 2023

    SpinOk SDK with spyware features found in apps with 421 million downloads

    Doctor Web found a marketing SDK in 101 Google Play apps that could list and upload files and read or replace clipboard contents. It says a later version dropped these functions. BleepingComputer reports all but one of the ten most-downloaded listed apps were removed.

    Code a developer adds for advertising can reach your files and clipboard, and the app itself gives no sign of it.

    Disputed: It is unclear whether app publishers knew what the SDK did. The 421 million figure is a total across all the apps, and some had the module only in certain versions.

    Source: Doctor Web · BleepingComputer

  19. February 23, 2023

    Mozilla finds most top apps' Google Play safety labels false or misleading

    Mozilla compared Data Safety labels with privacy policies for 40 top Google Play apps and found discrepancies in nearly 80%. It graded 16 apps Poor, 15 Needs Improvement and 6 OK; 3 did not fill in the form. Developers report the labels themselves.

    The label in the store is the developer's own declaration, and Google says apps are responsible for its accuracy.

    Disputed: Google pushed back, saying the researchers misunderstood the scope of the privacy policies. Mozilla replied that it used the policies each developer links from Google Play.

    Source: Mozilla Foundation · Security Boulevard

  20. December 1, 2022

    Phone makers' platform signing keys were found signing Android malware

    Google's Android security team listed ten malware samples signed with leaked platform keys, the keys that can give an app system-level access. Keys of Samsung, LG, Revoview and MediaTek were among those identified; the owners of some others were not.

    An app signed with such a key can ask for the same privileges as the system, with wide access to data on the phone.

    Disputed: Google said phone makers had acted to protect users and that it saw no sign of this malware on Google Play. Reports at the time said some of the leaked keys were still being used to sign apps.

    Source: BleepingComputer (on Google's Android partner report) · The Register

  21. April 6, 2022

    Google removes apps carrying a data-collecting SDK estimated on 60 million devices

    Researchers at AppCensus found an SDK from Panama's Measurement Systems in Google Play apps, taking phone numbers, email addresses and GPS data and able to read the clipboard. Apps with it were estimated on at least 60 million devices. Google removed dozens in March 2022.

    Ordinary apps, including prayer and speed-trap apps, carried this code, and its maker paid developers to include it.

    Disputed: Reports tied the company to a US defence contractor through corporate records and web registrations. Measurement Systems called this false and denied any such link.

    Source: SiliconANGLE (on the AppCensus findings) · Bitdefender
