What it checks

Every line on the label comes from your own phone.

Apps and their access

Every installed app: where it came from, its sensitive permissions, and the special access that lets an app read the screen or act for you.

Asks you forNothing

Trackers in the code

The code of every app is read on the phone and matched against the εxodus tracker list, and our own list of SDKs that share your connection, give remote support or record the screen.

Asks you forNothing

Genuine or modified VPN apps

VPN, proxy and circumvention apps are compared with their developers’ official signing keys. A copy is called modified only on firm evidence; an unknown key is shown, without a warning.

Asks you forNothing

The phone itself

USB and wireless debugging, certificate authorities you installed, the security patch, and the boot state from the phone’s hardware.

What you can do with it

A short plan, starting with the changes that remove the most risk. Each one opens the right Android screen, where you decide. Datapavan changes nothing itself.

Light

Apps that can control the phone, and apps not opened for 90 days that still hold a sensor, special access or VPN permission.

Medium

Also apps that can install apps, traffic sharing, USB debugging, and the other access of apps not opened for 90 days.

Strict

Also preinstalled apps, which Datapavan has not tested on your phone.

Journal

Records what actually changed, notices when a change comes back, and offers undo.

Safety floor

Leaves alone the apps your phone needs, such as your default apps and keyboard.

Questions

Does it send anything anywhere?

No. It has no permission to use the internet, so it cannot open a network connection. What it finds stays on the phone unless you share it yourself.

Will it work next to my VPN?

Yes. This version does not use Android’s VPN slot, so your VPN or circumvention tool keeps running as it is.

Does it block anything?

Not in this version. You make each change in Android’s settings. Blocking is planned for v0.3.

Why does it ask for usage access?

To read how much data each app used and when it was last on screen. You can refuse, and the rest still works. Only totals are read, and nothing is kept.

Which phones does it run on?

Phones from any maker with Android 10 or newer, 64-bit or 32-bit, with or without Google services. It is tested from Android 11.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free software under the Apache License 2.0, with no ads and no data sales.

Who makes it? Has anyone audited it?

One maintainer so far, and the code is public. Nothing has been independently audited yet.

The names

Both names come from Old Persian, the language of the Achaemenid kings, known almost only from inscriptions carved in stone about 2,500 years ago.

Datapavan

The app · DAH-tah-pah-vahn

dāta law + pāvan protector

Protector of the law

Dāta means law, what has been set down. It appears in Darius I's inscription at Behistun, and lives on in Persian as dād, justice. Pāvan means guardian: it is in xšaçapāvan, protector of the realm, the title that reached English as satrap. The compound itself is ours, built from those two attested parts. That data means data in English is deliberate wordplay, not a shared origin.

Visadahyu

The foundation, planned · vee-sah-DAH-hyoo

visa all + dahyu land, nation

Of all nations

The word comes from Xerxes I's inscription on the Gate of All Nations at Persepolis, which speaks of this gateway of all nations. The inscription is in three languages, and guardians stand at the gate's doorways. We chose the name for a planned foundation that belongs to no single nation or company. That the name opens with visa in English is wordplay too, not a shared origin: this gateway of all nations asks no one for a visa.
