# Spike: what an unprivileged app can read (M2)

- Date: 2026-10-29
- Question: which parts of the M2 audit, including the risk checks for VPN users, work for a normal app on stock Android without root?
- Method: a debug-only probe (branch `spike/m2-access-probe`, never merged) logs every check. Inert fixture components (accessibility service, notification listener, device admin, VPN service) and `adb` settings give it something to find. Each result was compared with `adb shell` (`appops`, `dumpsys`, `settings`, `pm`).
- Devices: Android 11 emulator (API 30, Google APIs), Android 17 emulator (API 37, 16 KB pages, Play image), and the Galaxy A40 (Android 11, One UI, security patch 2023-03). On the A40 the probe only read; no fixtures were set up there.

## Conclusions

- **M2 is feasible as designed**, including all VPN risk checks except naming the always-on VPN app on new Android versions. Instead, the audit names installed VPN apps, the ones the user has authorised, and whether a VPN is active.
- **Find components by permission, not by intent query.** VPN, accessibility and notification-listener services are found by reading each package's services and matching the binding permission; the intent query misses components that are not exported.
- **The real phone already shows why the audit matters:** a sideloaded messenger holds overlay, install-unknown-apps, SMS, microphone and contacts access, and a preinstalled Facebook system app sent the most data of all.
- **M3 and M4 are feasible:** other apps' APKs are readable for the DEX scan, and per-app byte counts work once the user grants usage access.
- **Permissions needed for v0.1** (decided in [ADR 0014](../decisions/0014-v0.1-permissions.md)): `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES`, `PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS` (granted by the user in Settings) and `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`. None of them allows network connections; `INTERNET` stays forbidden by the build.
- The attestation result must be parsed carefully. The certificate chain comes from the device itself, and a compromised system can tamper with a check on itself, so the UI states what was verified and how strongly.

## Later finding (same day)

On the A40, `GET_APP_OPS_STATS` has the protection level `signature|privileged|development`. Its
*development* flag means the user can grant it once over USB with `adb shell pm grant`. With it,
Datapavan can read Android's own per-app history of microphone and camera use and name the app
exactly (the optional advanced mode in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), v0.2). `WATCH_APPOPS`, which would
give live notifications, is `signature|privileged` only.

`DUMP` is also `signature|privileged|development`. Granted once over USB, it lets the app read
the audio and camera services' own logs (`Debug.dumpService`), which name the app behind every
recording and camera session. Read over USB on the A40, the audio log listed 50 recording
sessions, all from the Google app with the source `HOTWORD`, and one of them was active at that
moment: with "Hey Google" on, the Google app keeps the microphone open to listen for the wake
word. Kernel audio state (`/proc/asound`) is closed even to the USB shell, so true hardware
evidence needs stage 3 hardware.

Turning "Hey Google" off in the Google app ended that recording at once: the audio service then
listed no active recording. The settings page offered a separate "While driving" wake word, which
stays on unless it is turned off as well.
