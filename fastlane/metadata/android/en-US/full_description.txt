Datapavan shows what the apps on your phone can reach and how much they send, and guides you to the Android settings that limit it. Everything runs on the phone. It needs no root and no account, and it has no permission to use the internet at all.

<b>What it checks</b>
<ul>
<li>Every installed app: where it came from, its sensitive permissions and the special access that lets an app read the screen or act for you.</li>
<li>Trackers and SDKs inside each app's code, matched on the phone against the εxodus database and Datapavan's own list of SDKs that share your connection, give remote support or record the screen.</li>
<li>VPN, proxy and circumvention apps, checked against their developers' official signing keys, so that a modified copy shows.</li>
<li>The phone itself: USB and wireless debugging, certificate authorities you installed, the security patch, and the boot state from hardware key attestation.</li>
<li>How much data each app received and sent over mobile data and Wi-Fi, and which apps you have not opened for months but still hold access.</li>
<li>One activity at a time: read the counts, use an app, read again, and see what it sent while you only watched or scrolled.</li>
</ul>

<b>What it helps you do</b>
A short plan, the changes that remove the most risk first. Each one opens the right Android screen where you decide, and a journal on the phone keeps what you changed so that you can undo it. It leaves out the apps it recognises as essential.

<b>What it never does</b>
No analytics, no crash reports, no servers, no accounts. Datapavan itself sends nothing anywhere. Genuine Datapavan has no internet access and never asks for it: a copy that asks for it is not genuine.

Languages: English, French, German and Persian.
